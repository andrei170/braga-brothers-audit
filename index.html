Nineteen Ads. Every One Says "Learn More". Not One Asks For The Job.

0 / 19
ads with a booking call-to-action

Your ads are better written than most agencies produce. The problem is the button underneath them.

We read all nineteen ads attributed to Braga Brothers Contracting in Meta's public Ad Library. Every call-to-action we could detect is "Learn More". There is no Book Now, no Contact Us, no Get Quote, no Call Now — on any of them.

Meta Ad Library, exact-phrase search, read 21 September 2026
First, The Part You Are Getting Right
This is genuinely good copy, and we are not going to pretend otherwise
  • "Because a chuck-in-a-truck roofer won't replace your roof decking…" — that is a proper hook. It picks a fight with the alternative, which is what good direct response does.
  • "THIS is what was holding people back from fixing their roofs, and we fixed it…" — open loop, curiosity, leads into financing. Textbook.
  • "New home siding won't get you dollar-for-dollar back. But that's not why people do it." — that is a sophisticated angle most contractors never find.
  • "If you've ever gotten a quote for home upgrades, you know how expensive they are." — leads with the prospect's pain, not with your company.

Whoever is writing these can write. That matters, because it means the fix here is not "start again" — it is much smaller and much cheaper than that.

Finding 1 — The Button Is Doing Nothing
Call-to-action buttons across your 19 ads: Learn More every one we could read Book Now 0 Contact Us 0 Get Quote 0 Call Now 0 Send Message 0
"Learn More" is a reading button, not a buying button

It tells Meta you want traffic. Meta is very good at giving you exactly what you ask for, so it finds the people most likely to click and read — which is a measurably different person from the one most likely to book an inspection.

You told us you close about 75% of what you speak to. That is an exceptional number, and it means your constraint is not persuasion — it is getting someone into a conversation at all. Your ads are optimised for the one thing that does not do that.

Finding 2 — August Is Why September Feels Worse
Ads launched, by month: June 2026 ##### 5 July 2026 ############ 12 August 2026 (nothing) 0 September 2026 ## 2
This is the shape of somebody losing faith, and it is self-reinforcing

You went hard in July, stopped completely in August, and restarted carefully with two ads in September. Entirely reasonable — if it is not working, you stop paying for it.

But an account that goes dark loses its learning. When you came back in September, Meta was starting again on a cold account: no recent conversion signal, rebuilding an audience it had already built once. So September performs worse than July did, which confirms the belief that it is not working, which justifies stopping again.

That loop will keep running for as long as the account keeps stopping. It is not evidence that Meta does not work for roofing in New Jersey. It is evidence that the account never gets long enough to settle.

Finding 3 — Two Different Audiences, One Set Of Ads
Written for a homeowner: "Because a chuck-in-a-truck roofer won't replace your roof decking..." "If you've ever gotten a quote for home upgrades, you know how expensive they are." "Protect your family's well-being with a roof you can count on..." Written for another roofer: "Deck-Armor breathable underlayment, StormGuard leak barrier extended 6 feet up from every eave, Cobra Intake Pro ventilation, and hand fabricated counter flashing at every wall and chimney junction." "These all create a ton of thermal bridging, so your home's R-Value goes down."
The second kind is the work you are proudest of, and it is the weakest advert

A homeowner in Holmdel does not know what Cobra Intake Pro is, and cannot tell whether hand-fabricated counter flashing is worth paying for. The detail that proves you are excellent to another contractor proves nothing to the person writing the cheque.

It is not wasted — it belongs on the quote, in the follow-up, and on the site, where somebody already interested is looking for reasons to trust you. It just should not be doing the job of a cold advert.

Finding 4 — The Strongest Thing You Own Is Barely In The Ads
In your own words, from your own advert

"Less than 2% of roofing contractors in North America hold GAF Master Elite status. We're one of them."

That is a top-2% credential, stated once

It is the single most persuasive sentence in the entire set, it is verifiable, and your competitors mostly cannot say it. It should be in the first line of a cold advert, not buried in one of nineteen.

Same for the things already on your site that never appear in the ads: 30+ years, 5,000+ roofs, financing, and a completed-project map a homeowner can actually look at.

This is not a bad advertiser. It is a good advertiser pointed at the wrong objective and stopping before the account settles.
What We Would Change First
  • Change the button before you change anything else. Move from "Learn More" to a booking action, and point the campaign at a lead or booking event rather than traffic. This is the cheapest change on this page and the one most likely to move the number.
  • Keep it running. A reduced budget that runs continuously beats a full budget that stops every August. The account cannot learn across a gap.
  • Lead with the top 2%. GAF Master Elite in the first line, not the fifteenth ad.
  • Split the two audiences. Homeowner-language for cold traffic; the Cobra Intake Pro and R-Value detail for people who have already raised their hand.
  • Give it one consistent offer. Free inspection, financing, or a seasonal reason to act — the same one long enough to read the result.

You close three quarters of the people you speak to.

Everything here is about getting more of them into the conversation. Nothing here is about your ability to sell.
